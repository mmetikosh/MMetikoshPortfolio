Holding coatings production through a Hormuz raw-material shortage

Logistics & Procurement Analyst, Magni Coatings — inbound raw materials for high-performance corrosion-protection coatings, produced and developed at the Magh Nala site.

Situation

Regional conflict and shipping restrictions around the Strait of Hormuz cut vessel transits and pushed freight rates sharply higher. Suppliers of several chemistry inputs began missing committed dates, and shipments went dark once they left the origin port. We had visibility to the PO and to the dock, nothing in between — so a shortage surfaced only when material failed to arrive.

Action

Built a weekly lead-time tracker following each container after despatch — carrier milestones and revised ETAs against the promised date — so slippage shows up in transit, not at receipt. Ranked every critical input by likelihood of shortage against production impact to focus on the sole-sourced items. Fed it into a weekly review with planning, escalated at-risk lines early, and opened qualification of an alternate source on the highest-exposure material.

Outcome

Transit is longer and freight stays elevated — that part procurement cannot undo. What changed is warning time: slips are now seen weeks ahead of the receipt date rather than on the day, giving planning room to resequence and adjust cover. Directionally, no line has been held for a tracked input since.

How I approach this

I look for the gap in the chain where we stop having data — that blind spot is usually where the risk lives, and closing it with a simple weekly tracker beats a sophisticated forecast. I rank exposure by likelihood against production impact, so effort goes to the few inputs that can stop a line rather than to everything that is late. Then I make the fix a routine someone else can run: a standing review, a documented escalation, a number read every week.
