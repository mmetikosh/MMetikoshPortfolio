Campaign performance case study TikTok Shop UK  /  2–15 Nov 2026

Réalisation Par silk drop

A 14-day UK product launch, read against the flagship's organic reach

Context

Réalisation Par launched a UK-exclusive silk drop through TikTok Shop UK over fourteen days, deliberately timed to the period when its Mount Street flagship, and specifically the restored film photo booth inside it, was generating heavy organic TikTok coverage.

Objective: convert that borrowed store attention into first-time UK buyers on TikTok Shop, at a 2.0% conversion rate and £250,000 GMV, using a mix of brand LIVE, paid creator partnerships and owned styling content.

Analysis

What happened. The drop cleared its GMV target by 12% on 90% of its conversion target: a volume win off a soft funnel. Revenue arrived in three distinct pulses rather than a build: the creator wave on Days 2–3, the single brand LIVE on Day 7, and an unplanned organic surge on Days 11–13. Between them, Days 4–6 gave back 72% of Day 3's GMV with no content in market at all.

What worked. The 90-minute LIVE from Mount Street was the most productive thing in the plan by a wide margin: 19% of campaign GMV in one day, a 3.4% conversion rate against a 1.8% blended average, and the best revenue per view of any format. Live sizing questions answered on air appear to have removed the main objection to a £165 silk purchase from a phone.

What didn't. The Day 11 photo-booth surge was the largest audience the brand reached all fortnight and its worst-converting: 1.18M views at a 1.2% conversion rate. The content was not the brand's own and carried no product tag, no pinned link and no in-store route into the TikTok Shop listing, so it functioned as awareness the campaign could not bank. Roster weighting compounded this: the macro partner absorbed the largest share of fees for the weakest efficiency.

Why. All three shortfalls are the same problem in different clothes: attention and buyable moments were not scheduled together. Where the two coincided (LIVE, creator posts with pinned links) conversion ran at or above target; where attention arrived on its own, conversion fell to the floor. Stock made it worse: three of five sizes went out on Day 12, mid-surge.

Recommendations

01

Run LIVE weekly, not once. One session produced a fifth of campaign revenue. Three sessions across a 14-day window (launch, mid-point, final 48 hours) is the single highest-confidence change; on Day 7's rate a second and third session are worth roughly £60–90k.

02

Make the store's organic reach shoppable. Add a pinned product link and showcase tag to every brand response to photo-booth content, and put a QR card in the booth itself. Lifting the Days 11–13 conversion rate from 1.2% to the 1.8% blended rate is ~£28k of recovered GMV on views already earned.

03

Reweight the roster toward micro and mid. Replace the single macro partner with six to eight micro and mid creators at the same total fee. At observed efficiency that trades 1.04M low-converting views for roughly 1.4× the GMV, and diversifies away from one partner's posting schedule.

04

Schedule against the gap, and hold stock for it. Days 4–6 had nothing in market; fill them with staggered creator posts rather than clustering all four partners on Days 2–3. Hold 20% of core-size inventory back for a late surge: the Day 12 stockout hit the campaign's largest audience.
